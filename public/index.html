Recursion

 

Every hand ends in a smaller clock, turned to face along that hand. The heaviest branches turn by the hour angle, the mid-tone by the minute, the palest by the second. A pulse leaves the centre each second; its depth is the fraction of the second elapsed. Spatial, below the dial, opens the same clock in three dimensions. This dial is small, so it renders one level fewer than the Iter scale asks for.Time is applied as three nested rotations: the hour about Z, the minute about Y, the second about X. Every arm is the axle its two children turn about, so each joint is a right angle, and the generations repeat hour, minute, second at a smaller scale: hours heavy and deep, minutes plain, seconds fine and bright. At twelve o'clock every twist is zero and the tree is a perfect lattice: a dense core at midnight, opening until it fills the hour ring at noon. Tap a joint to travel to it. This screen is small, so it renders two generations fewer than a full-size one would for the same Iter setting.

A symbolic calendar, not a lunar one. Each sigil places a body on a twelve-fold orbit (30° per month), sets a symbolic phase that runs new → full → new over the year, and inscribes a seasonal triangle; equinox and solstice months carry an axis. Quadrants follow the boreal astronomical seasons. All twelve stand around the year ring: pull back, or choose Year, to find them.
